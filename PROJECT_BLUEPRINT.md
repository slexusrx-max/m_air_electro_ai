## 0. Executive Summary

M Air Electro AI is an international AI platform for:

- electrical diagnostics;
- repair guidance;
- verified electrical experts;
- safe purchase of electrical services and parts;
- marine and industrial electrical expertise.

The product must not become a general handyman marketplace.

The core product is AI diagnostics plus deterministic engineering tools.
The marketplace exists to support trusted execution after diagnosis, not to replace the technical identity of the platform.

The initial commercial launch should stay narrow enough to produce a reliable, legally safe, and technically strong MVP.

---

## 1. Product Vision

M Air Electro AI should become the trusted operating platform for electrical fault diagnosis, electrical calculations, technical document interpretation, expert matching, and protected service and parts transactions.

The platform should help users:

- understand electrical faults faster;
- reduce unsafe trial-and-error troubleshooting;
- move from symptom to diagnosis to repair decision;
- calculate key electrical parameters with deterministic tools;
- analyze photos, PDFs, manuals, drawings, and schematics;
- find verified electrical experts;
- buy electrical services and parts through a protected marketplace flow;
- solve marine and industrial electrical cases with premium technical depth.

The long-term vision is a platform where AI, engineering tools, documents, experts, and marketplace workflows reinforce each other in one product experience.

## 4. Problems Solved

The platform must solve these core problems:

### 4.1 Diagnostic Uncertainty

Users often know symptoms but not the root cause.
AI diagnostics and structured troubleshooting reduce confusion and wasted effort.

### 4.2 Unsafe Troubleshooting

Electrical faults carry safety risk.
The product must provide safety-aware guidance, escalation rules, and clear boundaries for non-professionals.

### 4.3 Fragmented Technical Workflow

Today, diagnosis, calculations, manuals, expert search, and service booking happen in separate tools.
M Air Electro AI combines them in one workflow.

### 4.4 Lack of Trust in Service and Parts Transactions

Users fear poor workmanship, hidden defects, wrong parts, fake parts, and payment disputes.
The platform provides verified experts, protected payment timing, evidence requirements, and dispute handling.

### 4.5 Weak Tooling for Marine and Industrial Cases

Marine and industrial electrical issues are too specialized for general service marketplaces.
This platform builds a differentiated premium vertical for those use cases.

### 4.6 Poor Technical Buying Decisions

Users often buy the wrong part or approve the wrong repair.
Diagnostics, calculators, documents, and expert escalation improve decision quality before payment.

## 9. Primary User Journeys

### 9.1 Home Electrical Fault Journey

1. User describes symptom.
2. AI diagnostics asks structured follow-up questions.
3. Platform returns likely causes and safe next steps.
4. User runs calculators or uploads photos/manuals if needed.
5. Platform suggests verified expert or electrical service request.
6. User books or pays through protected flow.

### 9.2 Parts Sourcing Journey

1. User identifies likely faulty board, breaker, relay, sensor, or other component.
2. User confirms requirements with diagnostics, documents, or expert help.
3. User browses verified parts listing.
4. Listing includes condition, testing notes, photos, serials, and shipping terms.
5. User pays through protected marketplace flow.
6. Release occurs after provider-confirmed delivery and inspection window rules.

### 9.3 Expert Consultation Journey

1. User reaches uncertainty or elevated risk.
2. Platform recommends verified expert based on domain and problem type.
3. User books consultation or remote diagnostics session.
4. Consultation result is saved with notes, recommendations, and related files.
5. User may proceed to repair, parts purchase, or further diagnostics.

### 9.4 Marine or Industrial Case Journey

1. User enters alarm, symptom, equipment type, or uploads technical documents.
2. Platform routes case to marine or industrial context.
3. AI diagnostics and documents analysis narrow the issue.
4. User runs relevant calculator or escalates to premium expert.
5. Platform supports service, consultation, or protected parts transaction if needed.

### 9.5 Seller or Expert Journey

1. Expert or seller applies for verification.
2. Platform reviews identity, qualifications, and payout readiness.
3. Approved profile becomes visible in marketplace or expert directory.
4. User receives requests, orders, or consultations.
5. Completion evidence and payment release follow platform rules.

## 10. Marketplace and Protection Workflow

### 10.1 Product Principle

The platform must not describe itself as a licensed escrow company.

It must instead operate as:

- a marketplace using a payment provider such as Stripe Connect or equivalent;
- a protected transaction workflow with delayed payout and platform-defined release conditions.

### 10.2 Payment Boundary Rules

Mandatory rules:

- do not store customer funds independently;
- do not independently transmit customer money outside provider capabilities;
- do not market the platform as an escrow institution;
- use provider-supported marketplace payment flows only;
- release timing must depend on provider capabilities plus platform rules.

### 10.3 Service Workflow

Recommended flow:

1. User places service request or accepts quote.
2. Payment provider authorizes or captures deposit.
3. Platform stores order state and evidence requirements.
4. Expert completes service.
5. Completion confirmation requires:
   - completion code or confirmation action
   - photo evidence where relevant
   - invoice or service summary
6. Short dispute window begins.
7. Payout release occurs according to platform rules and provider capabilities.

### 10.4 Parts Workflow

Recommended flow:

1. User purchases verified part.
2. Listing includes serial number, photos, condition notes, testing notes, and shipping policy.
3. Provider processes marketplace payment.
4. Shipping tracking is attached to order.
5. Delivery confirmation opens inspection window.
6. If no dispute is raised within allowed window, payout release proceeds under provider capabilities.

### 10.5 Dispute Workflow

Required components:

- dispute trigger by buyer;
- evidence upload by both parties;
- support or admin review state;
- structured outcome states:
  - buyer refund
  - partial refund
  - payout release
  - manual review escalation

### 10.6 Trust Layer Components

Trust mechanisms should include:

- verified experts;
- verified sellers;
- review system;
- evidence requirements;
- standardized listing metadata;
- dispute policy;
- controlled payout timing;
- optional warranty add-ons.

## 13. AI Provider Abstraction

The AI layer must remain provider-agnostic.

Recommended abstraction:

- `AI Gateway`
- `Provider Adapter`
- `Model Router`
- `Prompt Registry`
- `Context Builder`
- `Safety Layer`
- `Evaluation Layer`

Supported provider types:

- OpenAI;
- Anthropic;
- Google;
- self-hosted models later if needed.

Routing rules:

- diagnostics reasoning -> higher quality reasoning model;
- document Q&A -> retrieval plus document-capable model;
- image-assisted analysis -> multimodal model;
- low-latency assistant prompts -> lower-latency model;
- moderation or risk review -> dedicated moderation stack.

Non-negotiable AI rule:

- AI explains and guides;
- deterministic engines calculate;
- AI does not become the source of truth for formulas, payouts, or legal rules.

- do not generate unsafe electrical guidance without warnings and escalation logic;

### 22.5 Delivery Rules

- before adding a major feature, identify the bounded domain;
- before adding AI behavior, classify whether the task is deterministic, retrieval-based, or generative;
- before adding marketplace logic, define states, evidence, and dispute impact;
- before changing database models, preserve migration clarity and role compatibility;
- document major architecture decisions when they materially change the system.
